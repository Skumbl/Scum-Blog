## My thoughts on AI
AI isn't going away, not in software development, not in day-to-day life, and to say otherwise is to put Pandora back in her box. Despite all the scams and slop and useless people peddling AI tools that do nothing more inconveniently, there is real technology here that is too useful. It has completely taken over the development world and it makes me quite sad.

I've been really struggling with my inner thoughts about what it means to use AI in day-to-day development, and how I balance my own personal beliefs and convictions with a genuinely very useful service.

I think my biggest gripe is that AI makes me stop thinking, I feel like I'm doing autopilot work. I've always loved puzzle solving, this started with Zelda and Portal games when I was a kid, and then that spun into a life where I care very deeply about software development and my field. And I don't think that puzzle solving is there anymore when I use AI to debug anymore. I don't get those "OHHH" moments when I find a bug. And I understand that for my personal projects there is no one holding a gun to my head making me use an LLM, but there is A LOT of pressure right now to fall in line or fall behind.

### Where my beliefs and ethics fall on the matter

On principle on the current state of AI. I don't think models as they currently operate and are owned by private organizations are good. They are built on stolen data from humans that did not consent to having their data stolen. This is something that has always been important to me, I went out of my way for years to make sure that my data wasn't stolen from me without my express permission. The models themselves are quite good at software and other jobs given time and money, but I fear this ends justify the means mentality.

There is also the not so hidden cost to society at the moment that we are all aware of. I don't think we should be building high power datacenters with little to no oversight because we need our coding models to have 2% lower error rate. I don't think we should have AI datacenters taking up a bunch of water

I also don't think that we should be licensing out our ability to code and work on open source software for the betterment of everyone from companies that clearly don't have the interests of us open source developers in mind.

_"you will own nothing and you will be happy"_ marches on in America but now we will _know nothing and be happy_.

### My actions do not match my beliefs
I still use models at work because I am required to do so, but like many developers I take the skills I use from work and use them in my own personal projects. Is that an excuse? No, and I feel bad about it every time I do. I have this mental dissonance in my mind.

And as I've integrated AI tools into my personal projects, I have noticed that I am genuinely able to understand more broad, complicated concepts, as well as get more done. But it feels BAD. I don't want to brag to people that I used AI to learn something. I don't want to tell people I was stuck with a JavaScript issue so I asked an LLM to take a quick look.

My only solution to this as of late has been switching to open local models that don't use a data center, and they're good enough for my purposes. I can give a breakdown on my setup for that if anyone is interested. But I don't know what to do. I’m currently looking into different frameworks for how to actually _work_ with AI without losing that mental edge, like some of the stuff being discussed over at [0rwx](https://0rwx.com/), No Boiler Plate's mental model. I’m trying to find a way to use the tool without letting it replace the part of the process that makes me a dev.
